01 — The four ridings on the ballot

British Columbia's 93 MLAs are elected one per riding by first past the post. Richmond's four ridings have two Conservative incumbents seeking re-election (Teresa Wat, Steve Kooner) and two open seats: Richmond Centre (incumbent Hon Chan is not on the 2026 ballot) and Richmond-Steveston (incumbent Kelly Greene is not seeking re-election). 2024 results are from Elections BC as reported on each riding's Wikipedia article; 2026 ballots are from the Elections BC final candidate list.

Ballot counts are final. Nominations closed October 3, 2026. The Elections BC final candidate list is the source for every 2026 candidate name and party on this page — anything reported earlier in the campaign (including some party press releases that named candidates who ultimately did not file) has been corrected against that list.

04 — Key dates & how to vote

The writ was dropped September 22, 2026. British Columbia elects 93 MLAs, one per riding, by first past the post — your ballot lists the candidates for your riding only, and you mark one. The rules below come from Elections BC's 2026-provincial-election pages.

Sep 22Writ of election dropped — 93-riding snap election called
Oct 3Nomination deadline — final candidate lists published
Oct 14Provincial leaders' debate
Oct 16 – 21Advance voting — 8 a.m.–8 p.m., at any advance voting place, no reason needed
Oct 18Last day to request a vote-by-mail package online or by phone (1-800-661-8683)
Oct 24Final voting day (Saturday) — polls 8 a.m.–8 p.m. PT; completed mail ballots must be received by 8 p.m.
Until Oct 24, 4 p.m.Vote in person at any district electoral office (Mon–Fri 9–5, Sat 10–4; on advance days 8–8; closed Sep 30 and Oct 12)
Also on the ballot this fall: a separate municipal election was held October 17, 2026, electing Richmond's mayor, council and school trustees — see the municipal candidate guide. The provincial vote here is a different ballot, a different level of government, and a different set of candidates.

06 — Provincial polling (with its caveats)

There is no riding-level polling for any Richmond seat — every poll below is province-wide, which is not the same as a forecast for your local ballot. Figures are decided-plus-leaning where the pollster reports it, with undecided shown separately; CentreBC and OneBC are not reported by every firm. The table is chronological by field dates, not ranked, and no poll on this page is a prediction.

Read this first. The most recent reading — Angus Reid Institute, fielded Oct 1–3, 2026 (n=1,013 online, ±3 pts) — shows Conservative 48 / NDP 43 / Green 7 and is described by the firm as "Conservatives hold a five-point lead over a gaining NDP." A week earlier the Conservative lead was larger; since the writ dropped September 22 the NDP has narrowed it. In the 2024 election the NDP won a minority government and the Conservatives finished second; the 2026 snap election was called less than two years into that mandate. Treat all of this as context, not prediction — and remember your vote picks one MLA for one riding, not a province-wide result.
